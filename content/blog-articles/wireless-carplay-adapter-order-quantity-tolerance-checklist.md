## Direct answer: how should order quantity tolerance be controlled?

A wireless CarPlay adapter order quantity tolerance should be written into the purchase order before production begins. The clause should define whether any overrun or underrun is allowed, the approved range or decision method, which SKU and packaging level it applies to, who authorizes a variance, how accepted units are priced and paid, and what happens to surplus finished goods, shortfalls, branded materials and components.

“Ship the actual production quantity” is not a sufficient rule. A buyer needs a predictable receiving and inventory quantity; a supplier needs a documented decision path when yield, inspection disposition or pack-out creates a difference. The final accepted quantity must be reconciled by SKU, revision, lot, shipment and destination rather than hidden inside an invoice adjustment.

## What overrun, underrun and quantity tolerance mean

An **overrun** is finished saleable quantity above the ordered amount. An **underrun** or shortfall is accepted quantity below it. A **quantity tolerance** is the pre-approved boundary or decision rule for those differences.

These terms should not be confused with production output, inspection sample quantity, spare units or replacement stock. A factory may produce extra units to manage expected yield, but those units do not automatically become buyer-owned finished goods. Likewise, units held for destructive testing, golden samples, after-sales replacement or unresolved nonconformance should not be counted as normal saleable delivery unless the agreement says so.

## Decide whether tolerance is appropriate

Some programs require an exact quantity because inventory, launch allocation, retailer purchase orders or regulated labels are tightly controlled. Other programs may accept a small documented variance when production processes or custom packaging make exact output inefficient. There is no universal percentage that fits every project.

The decision should consider:

- sales forecast and channel commitments;
- minimum useful shipment or carton quantity;
- component and packaging purchase increments;
- expected process yield and inspection disposition;
- branded-material security;
- product revision and obsolescence risk;
- inventory carrying cost;
- payment and customs-document accuracy; and
- the feasibility of completing a shortfall later.

If any variance would create commercial harm, state “exact quantity required” and define how shortfall risk will be managed before production release.

## Apply the rule by SKU, not only by purchase order

A mixed purchase order can contain several hardware, firmware, connector, artwork or language variants. A total-order tolerance can hide a shortage in one critical SKU behind surplus in another.

| Control level | Question to answer | Risk if omitted |
|---|---|---|
| Saleable SKU | Is tolerance assessed separately for each exact configuration? | surplus of one variant masks shortage of another |
| Retail pack | Does the accepted count include completed approved packaging? | unfinished units are treated as ready inventory |
| Master carton | Must final quantities align with full-carton pack-out? | partial cartons create handling and count errors |
| Destination | Can one warehouse receive more while another receives less? | channel allocation changes without buyer approval |
| Shipment | Is tolerance evaluated per split shipment or only at final closure? | early variance is mistaken for final order completion |
| Replacement stock | Is service stock part of the order or a separate reserve? | after-sales coverage is consumed as sellable stock |

## Define the commercial decision matrix

The purchase order or supply agreement should explain the response to each condition.

| Actual accepted quantity | Buyer decision | Commercial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Exact ordered quantity | accept when all other release criteria pass | invoice and receive the ordered quantity |
| Within an approved overrun range | accept automatically or require written release, as agreed | invoice only accepted units at the agreed basis |
| Above the approved overrun range | hold excess pending buyer instruction | no automatic shipment, invoice or ownership transfer |
| Within an approved underrun range | accept and close, or require completion, as agreed | reconcile shortage and payment explicitly |
| Below the approved underrun range | corrective plan and buyer decision required | do not close the order without authorized disposition |
| Mixed revision or nonconforming surplus | quarantine and control separately | not counted as accepted saleable quantity |

Avoid retroactive approval by silence. Identify the buyer role that can approve a variance and the evidence required before shipment.

## Separate build quantity from accepted quantity

The factory may start more units than the purchase-order quantity to protect against yield loss, sampling or damage. Track at least these quantities separately:

1. material issued;
2. units assembled;
3. units completing production test;
4. units accepted after inspection;
5. units packed as the approved SKU;
6. units released for shipment;
7. units retained for approved purposes; and
8. scrap, rework, nonconforming and unexplained variance.

The invoice should be based on the contractually accepted quantity, not simply the largest production number. Traceable reconciliation prevents rejected or unfinished units from being billed as saleable goods.

## Control surplus branded goods and materials

Private-label housings, labels, manuals and cartons can carry the buyer’s identity. Surplus finished goods or packaging therefore create channel leakage, counterfeit confusion and obsolete-brand risk if they are not controlled.

Define whether excess items are:

- purchased by the buyer;
- held for a future approved order;
- converted to authorized service stock;
- reworked under a documented instruction;
- de-branded where feasible and permitted; or
- destroyed with suitable evidence.

State ownership, storage period, access, inventory reporting and final disposition. Do not allow branded surplus to enter another channel. ## Plan shortfall completion

When accepted quantity is below the agreed threshold, define whether the supplier must complete the missing balance, issue a credit, obtain order closure approval or provide another remedy. A small balance can be disproportionately expensive to manufacture and ship, so decide before production who bears repeat setup, inspection, documentation and freight costs.

If the shortfall will ship later, create a controlled balance with the same SKU, revision, packaging and traceability expectations. Do not silently substitute a newer firmware, different accessory or revised artwork. Use the [partial shipment and delivery allocation checklist](/blog/wireless-carplay-adapter-partial-shipment-delivery-allocation-checklist/) for the later release.

## Align inspection and counting evidence

Quantity reconciliation needs more than a packing-list total. Define the counting method appropriate to the product and pack-out: unit count, tray count, sealed inner-box count, carton count, weight cross-check, serial export or another controlled combination.

Inspection should confirm identity and condition as well as count. A carton containing the right number of units with the wrong firmware or label is not an acceptable quantity. Retain carton maps, serial or lot records where required, inspection disposition and release approval.

Any rework after counting should trigger an updated reconciliation. Prevent double counting between finished-goods stock, hold areas, sample cabinets and shipment staging.

## Connect payment and shipping documents

State whether deposits are calculated from ordered value or another milestone, and how the final payment reflects accepted quantity. Commercial invoice, packing list, customs data and receiving record should agree with the units actually released.

For an overrun, buyer acceptance should precede invoice and shipment. For an underrun, the invoice should not present the ordered quantity as delivered. If freight or other charges change with quantity, show the agreed allocation rather than concealing it in unit price.

## Close the order with a signed reconciliation

At order closure, compare purchase-order quantity, approved revisions, accepted overrun or underrun, shipped quantity, received quantity, claims, replacements, credits and remaining branded materials.

A closure record should identify:

- final accepted quantity by SKU;
- authorization for every variance;
- shipment and destination allocation;
- invoice and payment adjustment;
- open shortfall or replacement commitment;
- disposition of surplus product and buyer-branded material; and
- inventory records updated by both parties.

The order is not fully closed while an unexplained surplus, missing balance or branded-material obligation remains open.

## Buyer checklist before purchase-order release

- Decide whether exact quantity or a controlled tolerance is allowed.
- Apply the rule separately to every saleable SKU and revision.
- Define whether full-carton pack-out affects acceptance.
- Name the authority who can approve a variance.
- Separate production, accepted, packed and shipped quantities.
- Define automatic, conditional and prohibited overrun outcomes.
- Define underrun completion, credit and order-closure rules.
- Control surplus branded finished goods and packaging.
- Agree the counting, traceability and inspection evidence.
- Align invoice, packing list, customs and receiving quantities.
- Assign repeat setup, inspection and freight costs for a shortfall.
- Complete a final signed quantity and material reconciliation.

## FAQ

### Is there a standard order quantity tolerance for adapters?

No. Set the rule from the product, packaging, forecast, yield, inventory and commercial risk. Do not copy a universal percentage into every purchase order.

### Must a buyer pay for every extra unit produced?

Not automatically. Payment should follow the agreed tolerance, buyer authorization and accepted saleable quantity.

### Can surplus units become service stock?

Yes, if both parties authorize the allocation and the units match the controlled service configuration and traceability requirements.

### How should a shortfall be completed?

Define whether the balance is produced and shipped later, credited or formally closed, including responsibility for repeat setup, inspection and freight.

### What happens to excess private-label packaging?

The agreement should define ownership, secure storage, reporting, reuse, de-branding or destruction and the evidence required for final disposition.
