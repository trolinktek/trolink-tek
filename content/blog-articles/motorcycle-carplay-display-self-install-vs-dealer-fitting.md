# Motorcycle CarPlay Display Installation: Self-Install Kit vs Dealer Fitting

Offer a motorcycle CarPlay display as a self-install kit when the buyer can identify the exact mount and manufacturer-approved power connection before ordering, and the box contains the parts and instructions for that configuration. Offer dealer fitting when a technician needs to inspect the cockpit, identify the permitted power route or work behind bodywork. Use a pre-sale fitment review when the evidence is incomplete. For a powersports dealer, the choice determines what the customer is buying: a complete kit for a documented application or a fitted display with a recorded handover. The same screen can support both offers, provided each has a clear release condition, an installation owner and a support path if the first setup fails.

## Define what the customer is buying

A **self-install kit** is a display sold with the mount, power components, instructions and exclusions for a stated application. The buyer performs the installation within that documented scope. If the listing requires the buyer to find an unspecified bracket or identify a motorcycle circuit without guidance, the listing has left a key part of the purchase unresolved. A kit-only checkout works best when the evidence needed to select the correct box is available before payment.

A **dealer-fitted offer** combines the equipment with an agreed installation scope. The dealer confirms the application, specifies included parts and labor, and records a stationary handover. A job may start with an inspection if the vehicle has modifications or unclear accessory preparation. Set that expectation at intake, especially if a customer wants to order the hardware before the motorcycle reaches the workshop.

A **fitment review** is an intermediate sales path. It asks for just enough evidence to confirm a kit, scope a workshop job or decline the application. It can take the form of a photo review or a physical inspection. Use the comparison below to decide who should check the application and what the customer can order.

## Compare the offers at the point of sale

| Decision point | Boxed self-install | Dealer fitting | Fitment review first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mount and power selection | Exact parts and approved power route are documented for the buyer's configuration. | Technician confirms the assembly and power route within the quoted scope. | Request the missing cockpit photo, accessory connector identification or in-person inspection. |
| What the order includes | Display, specified hardware, instructions and a defined first support contact. | Equipment, specified installation work and a handover record. | A review or inspection with a defined outcome before parts and labor are committed. |
| Where the cost shows up | Hardware price, any explicitly required extra part, customer time and retailer support. | Hardware, labor, approved extra parts and any disclosed inspection charge. | Time to review evidence, with any charge explained before the review. |
| What stops the sale | Missing parts, unclear instructions, or an application outside the documented scope. | No supported installation method or a job the shop cannot responsibly perform. | Inconclusive evidence after review or no qualified local service option. |
| First support contact | Seller checks kit identity, missing items and setup steps, then routes product faults. | Fitting shop checks installation and handover record, then escalates suspected hardware faults. | The reviewer documents the decision so the buyer can continue without repeating intake. |

The best comparison is between two offers for the same vehicle and rider job. Quote the same display and intended use on both, then show where the kit or labor differs. If a camera, remote or quick-release mount changes the harness, hardware or work, quote that configuration separately. Otherwise the customer may compare two prices for materially different outcomes.

## Decide which path an order can take

**Release a self-install kit** when the customer can show the stated application and the required configuration is supported by the exact product documentation. A make and model alone can leave the cockpit or connector uncertain after owner modifications. Ask for the smallest useful confirmation, such as a view of the intended mounting point and the accessory preparation shown in the listing. Match the instruction revision to the parts shipped. If a buyer would need to improvise an electrical or mechanical connection to complete the job, stop the kit release.

**Offer workshop fitting** when a supported installation is plausible and the open questions require a qualified technician to inspect the motorcycle. That can include an uncertain cable route, aftermarket equipment in the intended mounting space, or a power arrangement that requires vehicle-specific service information. Confirm what the technician can inspect at intake and how the customer will approve a revised scope before work continues. A workshop booking is useful only if the shop has the information and capabilities for the proposed job.

**Hold the order for review** when neither route is ready. An unconfirmed setup can become a clear yes after a photo or an inspection, while an excluded setup may need another product entirely. Keep the two statuses separate in the order system. For a rider whose motorcycle already has factory navigation hardware, the [motorcycle display versus factory TFT navigation comparison](/blog/motorcycle-carplay-display-vs-factory-tft-navigation/) can help determine whether there is a worthwhile job for a second display.

Manufacturer guidance illustrates why these decisions stay tied to the exact hardware. CHIGEE documents different arrangements for its standard displays and compatible cradle versions, and tells buyers to check the mounting envelope and cable reach for the complete assembly. Its electrical guidance also distinguishes the supported main power connection from ignition detection on models that use a three-wire harness. Use the current instructions for the actual display and motorcycle before offering a connection method. [CHIGEE mounting guidance](https://support.chigee.com/en/articles/17238721-choose-a-mount-and-check-fit-for-your-chigee-display) and [CHIGEE power guidance](https://support.chigee.com/en/articles/17238774-connect-power-and-acc-to-your-chigee-display).

## Use one intake record for retail and service

Give the parts counter, e-commerce team and workshop the same fields. An intake record should explain how the retailer reached its decision. A later return can then be compared against the parts and scope originally agreed with the customer.

| Record field | What to capture | Who confirms it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application | Motorcycle make, model year, trim and relevant cockpit or accessory modifications | Sales records the customer's evidence and the workshop confirms changes where needed. |
| Proposed configuration | Display SKU, mount kit, power harness and optional parts with current instruction revision | Parts team matches the physical kit against the approved configuration. |
| Decision and reason | Self-install, fitting quote, pending review or outside supported scope | Sales or service assigns the path and names the evidence behind it. |
| Handover or first setup | Supported phone and helmet-audio path, confirmed settings and the relevant setup or release record | Seller provides setup instructions or installer records the demonstrated functions. |
| First issue and escalation | What failed, where it happened and who receives the case next | Seller or installer separates missing parts, fitment, setup and suspected product faults. |

For an installed job, capture the actual configuration and check the agreed functions with the motorcycle stationary. Document what was demonstrated, such as expected startup and shutdown, pairing with the intended phone and the selected audio path. Record any optional function outside the agreed scope. The [motorcycle display handover guide for tour fleets](/blog/motorcycle-carplay-guided-tour-fleet-handover/) shows how the handover changes when multiple riders use the same bike. If the shop used a demonstration phone, follow the product instructions to clear that pairing and show the customer how to reconnect their own device.

For a boxed sale, put the same SKU and mount identifiers on the listing, order and support record. State which help channel the customer should use if the parts differ from the guide or the setup cannot be completed. Frontline staff can ask for the kit ID, the installation stage and the symptom before assigning a replacement. That gives a returned display a meaningful history and helps the retailer decide whether to revise the kit, listing or training.

## Example: two orders for the same screen

Suppose a dealer receives two requests for a navigation display. The first rider has a motorcycle configuration already reviewed by the dealer and can identify the approved accessory preparation and mount location. The rider wants the standard display with the documented kit parts and will perform the work within the supplied instructions. The parts counter can confirm the configuration, release the box and explain the first setup contact.

The second rider has the same model year but an aftermarket windscreen and existing cockpit accessories. The dealer cannot confirm the assembly clearance or cable route from the initial inquiry. The service desk books an inspection, explains that parts and labor will be confirmed after review, and holds the hardware order until the plan is approved. If a supported fit is confirmed, the shop quotes the job and documents the handover. If no supported option is available, it closes the request with the reason.

The dealer may use the same display part number for both orders. The differences lie in who confirms the application, when the order is released and what evidence follows the transaction. Review those differences by offer when planning a pilot. Record the questions that stop checkout, why installation quotes change, and which support cases can be resolved from the intake record. Use that evidence to decide where to expand the self-install offer and where to keep a workshop path.

## Where the comparison stops

This guide compares retail offers for aftermarket motorcycle displays. The exact mounting, wiring and use limits come from the product and vehicle documentation. Unresolved interference with steering, controls or instruments, unclear electrical instructions, or a requested integration outside the approved scope should stop release until a qualified party resolves the issue. A confirmed fit on one bike gives the dealer evidence for that recorded setup. Recheck changes to the cockpit, mount, harness or display revision before extending the offer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a wireless motorcycle CarPlay screen automatically suitable for self-installation?

Wireless projection describes the phone connection. Check the display's separate power and mounting requirements, the approved kit parts and the exact motorcycle configuration before offering self-install.

### Can a dealer sell the same display as both a kit and a fitted option?

Yes, when each offer clearly identifies the configuration, included parts, scope of work and first support contact. Keep distinct order descriptions if the fitted offer includes different hardware or services.

### What should a customer send for a pre-sale fitment review?

Request model year, trim and relevant modifications, plus clear stationary photos of the intended cockpit area and any accessory preparation the customer can identify safely. Refer unresolved electrical identification to the workshop.

### Should a workshop give a fixed installation quote before seeing the bike?

Quote a defined scope when the application is documented and repeatable. If bodywork, existing accessories or the power route are uncertain, explain the inspection step and how any change to the quote will be authorized.

### How should staff handle a display that fails during first setup?

Start with the order configuration and the stage where setup stopped. Route fitment, missing parts, installation questions and suspected device faults to the named owner. Use the approved support process to establish whether a replacement is warranted.
