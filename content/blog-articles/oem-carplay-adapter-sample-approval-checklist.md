**Direct answer:** approve an OEM CarPlay adapter sample only after its physical unit, hardware revision, firmware build, accessories, branding, packaging and test evidence are tied to one controlled configuration. The approval record should state what was tested, what was not tested, which changes remain open and whether the sample is released for pilot production, quotation confirmation or a purchase order. A working demonstration alone is not a complete approval.

For an importer, distributor or private-label brand, the sample is the bridge between a supplier proposal and a commercial commitment. It should convert marketing statements into verifiable requirements. If the sample identity is incomplete, the buyer can test one configuration and later receive another under the same model name.

This checklist helps purchasing, product, quality and marketing teams reach one release decision without inventing universal compatibility or performance claims.

## Define what the sample is supposed to prove

Start with a written sample objective. Different sample stages answer different questions:

| Sample stage | Main decision | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Platform sample | Is the basic architecture relevant? | Product identity, core functions, initial vehicle checks |
| Engineering sample | Does the proposed configuration meet the requirement brief? | Hardware and firmware identity, issue log, targeted validation |
| Branded sample | Is the customer-facing product correct? | Logo, finish, boot behavior, accessories and label proof |
| Packaging sample | Is the sellable pack accurate and protected? | Artwork revision, manual, barcode, inserts and pack-out |
| Pilot sample | Can the approved configuration be produced and inspected? | Batch identity, production tests, inspection results and release record |

Do not call every unit a “golden sample.” A golden reference should represent a specifically approved state and remain protected from casual use or modification.

## Freeze the sample identity before testing

Record the sample before it enters a vehicle:

- buyer project and retail SKU;
- supplier model and sample ID;
- hardware or PCB revision;
- firmware build and configuration date;
- chipset or platform identification where agreed;
- enclosure version, finish and connector type;
- cable, converter and accessory identities;
- quantity received and physical condition;
- packaging and manual revision, if included;
- sender, receiver and receipt date.

Photograph the unit and its labels. Preserve the firmware version screen or supplier release record where available. If the product has no accessible version interface, agree on another traceable identification method.

A filename such as “latest firmware” is not sufficient. The buyer needs a stable build ID that can be referenced in test results and the purchase order.

## Compare the sample with the approved requirement brief

Convert the RFQ into an acceptance matrix. Each requirement should have a status: confirmed, failed, conditionally accepted, not tested or not applicable.

Review at least:

- intended product category and use case;
- prerequisite for factory wired CarPlay or Android Auto;
- supported phone platform stated for this model;
- vehicle USB data interface and supplied connector path;
- first pairing, automatic reconnection and phone switching;
- music, calls, microphone, navigation prompts and voice assistant;
- touchscreen, rotary controller and steering-wheel controls where applicable;
- reverse-camera interruption and return to the prior session;
- reset, update and recovery method;
- housing dimensions, cable reach and installation space;
- packaging, manuals and support destination;
- target-market labels and compliance review status.

## Establish a direct wired baseline

Before inserting a wireless adapter, connect a compatible phone directly to the same vehicle data port with a known data-capable cable. Confirm that factory wired CarPlay launches and that the relevant controls, audio paths and camera transitions function.

This baseline separates vehicle or phone limitations from adapter behavior. Record:

- vehicle make, model, year and regional trim;
- infotainment or head-unit version where available;
- USB port location and connector type;
- phone model and operating-system version;
- direct wired result;
- adapter result under the same conditions.

Do not convert a small test matrix into an “all vehicles” claim. The approval should identify tested combinations and describe the remaining compatibility risk.

## Test repeatability, not one successful connection

A sample that connects once has demonstrated a single event. A commercial product needs a predictable routine under the agreed scope.

Repeat cold starts, short-stop restarts and ordinary reconnections. Observe usable display, audio, controls and recovery rather than recording only the first logo or Bluetooth event. Include practical state changes such as the phone leaving range, Wi-Fi or hotspot activity, an incoming call and a reverse-camera interruption when relevant.

The same phone, vehicle, firmware and procedure should be used when comparing samples. Record failures and recoveries, not only successful cycles. If a problem appears, preserve its evidence before resetting or updating the unit.

## Review firmware ownership and update controls

Sample approval should answer:

- Which build was tested?
- Does it match the proposed hardware?
- Who can authorize a new build?
- How are release notes provided?
- How does the buyer verify a completed update?
- What recovery route applies after interruption or failure?
- Can different batches require different firmware?

Do not install an update during testing without preserving the original version and issue evidence. A new build creates a new test state and may require regression testing.

## Approve the physical and branded product

Functional approval does not automatically release the customer-facing design. Review:

- enclosure shape, dimensions and assembly gaps;
- color, texture, finish and approved logo position;
- indicator behavior and visibility;
- USB connector orientation and mechanical fit;
- cable length, strain relief and data capability;
- printed model, serial, batch or regulatory information;
- boot screen or interface customization where included;
- reset access and user instruction alignment.

Compare measurable items with controlled drawings or specifications. Photographs are useful evidence but can hide scale, color and surface differences.

If branding is not present on the technical sample, state that clearly. Do not let a technical function approval silently release an unseen logo, housing finish or label.

## Keep packaging approval separate but connected

The sellable pack must match the approved product. Review artwork and a physical pack-out for:

- correct model and product description;
- compatibility prerequisites and restrained claims;
- barcode and channel data;
- accessories and connector diagrams;
- manual language and support contact;
- product protection and movement inside the box;
- carton quantity and shipping marks;
- revision identity and obsolete-material handling.

## Record defects, deviations and open actions

Use an issue log instead of scattered messages. Each item should identify the sample, test condition, expected behavior, observed behavior, reproduction status, evidence, owner, target action and closure decision.

Possible disposition states include:

- accepted as tested;
- accepted with a documented limitation;
- corrective sample required;
- additional evidence required;
- packaging or documentation correction required;
- rejected for the proposed program.

Conditional approval must name the condition and the person who will verify closure. A promise to “fix in production” is not a closed action unless the change, verification and release responsibility are defined.

## Link approval to quotation and purchase order

Before commercial release, align the quotation and purchase order with the sample:

- buyer SKU and supplier model;
- hardware and firmware revision;
- included accessories;
- branding and packaging revisions;
- quantity and price assumptions;
- sample or pilot status;
- inspection and evidence requirements;
- change-notification obligations;
- destination markets and agreed documentation;
- warranty and after-sales route.

The purchase order should not rely only on a model name. Reference the approved specification and sample record so production, inspection and repeat orders share the same baseline.

## OEM CarPlay adapter sample approval checklist

- Define the sample stage and decision it supports.
- Assign a unique sample ID.
- Record hardware, firmware, enclosure and accessories.
- Compare the unit with the requirement brief.
- Prove the direct wired vehicle baseline.
- Test pairing, reconnection, audio, calls and controls.
- Record the vehicle, phone and software matrix.
- State the limits of compatibility evidence.
- Review firmware update and recovery controls.
- Approve physical appearance and branding separately.
- Match packaging, labels and manuals to the unit.
- Log deviations and assign closure owners.
- Define conditional approval explicitly.
- Reference the approved configuration in the quotation and PO.
- Preserve a controlled reference sample and approval record.
- Require notice before later configuration changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is an OEM CarPlay adapter sample ready for approval?

It is ready when its identity is traceable, agreed requirements have recorded results, open issues have a defined disposition and the approval scope states whether it releases another sample, pilot production or a purchase order.

### Is one working vehicle enough to approve compatibility?

No universal number applies, but one vehicle proves only that tested combination. Build a representative matrix for the target market and state what remains unverified.

### Should a buyer approve a sample before packaging is complete?

Technical approval can precede final packaging approval if the two decisions are separated. The sellable configuration should not be released until packaging, labels, accessories and manuals match the approved product.

### What should happen if firmware changes after approval?

Identify the new build, explain the change, assess risk, test the intended correction and relevant regressions, and issue a new or amended approval before production release.

### Is a golden sample enough for mass production?

No. It is a useful physical reference, but production also needs controlled specifications, firmware identity, test criteria, packaging files, inspection methods and batch traceability.

### What should be attached to the purchase order?

Reference the approved specification, sample or approval ID, hardware and firmware, accessories, packaging and manual revisions, inspection requirements and change-control terms.
