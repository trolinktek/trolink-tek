## Direct answer: what should a supplier deviation request contain?

A wireless CarPlay adapter supplier deviation request should identify the exact requirement that cannot be met, explain the reason, define the affected product, quantity, lot and time window, provide technical and commercial risk evidence, propose containment and verification, and state how normal production will be restored. The buyer should approve, reject or conditionally approve the request **before affected units are produced or shipped**. Approval must be limited, traceable and closed; it is not a permanent specification change.

For distributors, importers and private-label brands, a deviation can protect delivery when a temporary issue is understood and controlled. It can also quietly introduce an untested component, firmware, cable, label, packaging material or process into the market. The commercial decision therefore needs engineering, quality, regulatory, channel and after-sales input—not only a promise that the substitute is “equivalent.”

## Deviation, change and nonconformance are different

Use consistent terms before reviewing the request.

**Deviation request:** advance permission to depart temporarily from an approved requirement within a defined scope.

**Engineering change:** an intended revision to the controlled product or process that may become the new baseline after validation and approval.

**Nonconformance:** a discovered condition that already fails a requirement and needs containment, review and disposition.

**Concession or use-as-is decision:** buyer-authorized acceptance of a specific nonconforming quantity under defined conditions, where permitted by the agreement and applicable requirements.

| Situation | Correct control | Main buyer question |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary alternative proposed before build | deviation request | can this limited scope be accepted safely and commercially? |
| Permanent component or firmware revision | engineering change | what validation and document updates establish the new baseline? |
| Defect found after production | nonconformance process | what is affected, contained and dispositioned? |
| Finished lot requested for acceptance | concession/use-as-is review | what evidence supports this specific disposition? |

Do not relabel a permanent cost reduction as a temporary deviation. Conversely, do not create a permanent drawing revision for a one-time approved packaging substitution unless the buyer intentionally changes the baseline.

## Require a complete scope

An approver cannot evaluate “use an alternative part for this order.” The request should link the commercial order to the technical configuration and define:

- buyer and supplier names plus responsible contacts;
- purchase order, buyer SKU and supplier production identity;
- approved drawing, BOM, firmware, specification or packaging requirement;
- exact clause, characteristic or item proposed for deviation;
- proposed alternative and the technical difference;
- reason and root condition behind the request;
- affected quantity, serial or lot range and production site;
- planned start, end and expiration;
- inventory already built, in process or awaiting shipment; and
- requested decision date and delivery impact.

If the affected population cannot be bounded, place the relevant material and production on hold until traceability is restored. A request with an open-ended date or “all future orders” is a change proposal, not a controlled temporary deviation.

## Evaluate technical and customer risk

The supplier should compare the proposed condition with the approved baseline, not merely attach a substitute datasheet. For a wireless CarPlay adapter, the evaluation can include physical fit, USB connector geometry, power behavior, PCB or component function, firmware compatibility, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi identity, vehicle recognition, pairing, reconnection, audio, calls, controls, thermal behavior and recovery.

| Deviation area | Potential business impact | Evidence to request |
|---|---|---|
| Electronic component | boot, radio, power, reliability or compliance scope | comparison, sample validation and applicable requalification |
| Firmware or configuration | compatibility, naming, update or support behavior | build identity, change notes and regression evidence |
| Cable or connector | fit, data stability, retention and field returns | dimensional, functional and durability evidence |
| Enclosure or finish | assembly, appearance, thermal path or branding | controlled samples, inspection criteria and photos |
| Label or packaging | channel rejection, traceability or legal information | artwork comparison, scan checks and pack-out review |
| Manufacturing process | yield, hidden defects or consistency | process comparison, capability and inspection plan |

Avoid unsupported declarations such as “same specification” or “no impact.” State what was compared, by whom, against which criteria and with what result. If a required certification, legal marking or market obligation may be affected, route the decision to qualified compliance or legal reviewers; a commercial deviation approval cannot waive applicable law.

## Separate risk evidence from delivery pressure

Urgency is a planning input, not proof of acceptability. Record delivery consequences—such as a missed vessel, stockout or launch delay—separately from product risk. This lets management compare options without asking engineering to weaken its conclusion.

Useful options can include:

1. reject the deviation and accept the schedule impact;
2. approve a smaller pilot quantity pending further evidence;
3. split shipment between approved and deviation-controlled units;
4. use alternative inventory for a restricted market or channel;
5. approve only after additional inspection or functional testing; or
6. convert the request into a formal permanent change project.

The buyer should also evaluate service impact. A small material difference can create a second support configuration, spare-stock requirement, firmware branch or replacement rule. Capture those costs before approving an apparently simple schedule recovery.

## Define validation and acceptance evidence

Agree on evidence before the affected units are released. The plan should identify samples, configuration, test method, acceptance criteria, reviewer and record location. Depending on risk, evidence may include supplier data, incoming inspection, dimensional checks, controlled functional tests, vehicle validation, packaging trials, reliability work or a first-article review.

Use the [wireless CarPlay adapter factory testing checklist](/blog/wireless-carplay-adapter-factory-testing-checklist/) to map functional evidence and the [incoming quality inspection guide](/blog/wireless-carplay-adapter-incoming-quality-inspection/) to plan receiving controls. Neither creates universal pass limits; the approved product specification and buyer requirements remain the reference.

If testing uses a reduced scope because time is limited, state the residual risk. A passing sample supports only the tested configuration and method. It does not prove every vehicle, phone, market or production unit.

## Control segregation and traceability

Approved deviation units must remain identifiable through production, inspection, shipment and after-sales support. Define how the supplier will segregate material and how the buyer will recognize the received population.

Traceability can include lot codes, serial ranges, work-order references, controlled labels, carton records, inspection reports and deviation numbers. Avoid adding customer-visible markings without approval. The goal is to identify the configuration without confusing retail branding or violating channel rules.

The release record should reconcile:

- quantity authorized under the deviation;
- quantity produced and passed;
- quantity rejected, reworked or scrapped;
- quantity shipped by invoice and carton range;
- remaining affected material; and
- destination of every released lot.

Do not mix approved and deviation units in one unlabeled inventory pool. If field behavior differs, support teams must be able to identify which configuration a customer owns.

## Write a bounded approval decision

An approval should name the exact scope, evidence and conditions. Recommended fields include decision status, authorized quantity or lot, approved markets or customers, required additional tests, labeling or traceability method, shipment documents, pricing or warranty implications, expiration and responsible approvers.

Possible decisions are **approved**, **approved with conditions**, **rejected**, or **more evidence required**. Silence is not approval. A supplier should not proceed because a buyer failed to respond before the production deadline.

Where roles allow, separate the requester, technical reviewer, quality reviewer and commercial approver. The buyer’s authority matrix should determine who can accept product risk, compliance impact, delivery compromise or added cost. Preserve all attachments with the final decision so later reviewers see the same evidence.

## Close the deviation and restore the baseline

Every temporary approval needs an end. At expiration, confirm that the supplier returned to the approved component, firmware, artwork, packaging or process. Reconcile leftover alternative material and prevent it from entering later orders without authorization.

Closure should answer:

- Was the authorized quantity exceeded?
- Did inspection, production or field evidence reveal new risk?
- Was every affected shipment traceable?
- Is the original approved configuration restored?
- Does the proposed alternative need permanent change evaluation?
- Were drawings, BOMs and supplier records kept consistent?

If the alternative is to continue, initiate normal change control and revalidation. The [repeat-order change-control guide](/blog/private-label-carplay-adapter-repeat-order-change-control/) explains how to protect the approved baseline across future purchases. A successful temporary lot is useful evidence, but it does not automatically approve permanent use.

## Supplier deviation request checklist

- Identify the exact unmet approved requirement.
- Reference the purchase order, SKU and controlled revision.
- Describe the proposed condition and difference from baseline.
- Bound the affected quantity, lots, site and dates.
- State the reason and delivery consequences separately.
- Compare technical, quality, compliance and channel risks.
- Provide representative validation evidence.
- Define additional inspection and acceptance criteria.
- Segregate affected material and finished units.
- Assign traceability through shipment and after-sales.
- Record authorized approvers, conditions and expiration.
- Reconcile quantities and verify return to baseline.

## FAQ

### Is a supplier deviation the same as a product change?

No. A deviation is normally temporary and narrowly scoped. A permanent revision requires formal change control, validation and updated controlled documents.

### Can email approval replace a deviation form?

Only if the buyer’s controlled process accepts it and the message contains the complete scope, evidence, conditions, authority and record retention. A casual “okay” is inadequate.

### Can delivery urgency justify approval?

Urgency can influence the business decision, but it does not prove that product, compliance or customer risk is acceptable.

### Should deviation units have separate traceability?

Yes. The buyer and supplier should be able to identify affected lots through production, shipment and support without creating unauthorized customer-facing marks.

### What happens when the deviation expires?

Confirm the approved baseline is restored, reconcile remaining material and close the record. Continued use requires a new authorization or permanent change process.
