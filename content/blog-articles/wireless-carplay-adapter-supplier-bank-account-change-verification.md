## Direct answer: how should a buyer verify changed bank details?

When a wireless CarPlay adapter supplier asks a buyer to use new bank details, the buyer should pause payment, treat the change as unverified and confirm it through an independent process. Verify the requesting legal entity, beneficiary name, bank country, currency, invoice and purchase order; contact a known supplier representative through a trusted channel already on file; require authorized internal review; and release payment only after the change record is complete.

Do not verify a bank change by replying to the same message that announced it. A convincing email thread, familiar signature or urgent shipping explanation can still be compromised. The safe control is separation: the request, independent verification, approval and payment release should not depend on one person or one communication path.

This checklist is an operational purchasing control, not legal, banking or cybersecurity advice. Buyers should align it with their bank, insurer, finance policies, contracts and applicable regulations.

## Why adapter orders create a sensitive payment moment

Private-label electronics orders can involve deposits, balance payments, packaging charges, tooling, inspection, freight and repeat replenishment. A payment instruction may arrive during a busy milestone—sample approval, production release or shipment readiness—when urgency feels credible. That makes an unexpected beneficiary or account change easy to rationalize.

The risk is not limited to a completely unknown account. A request may change only one field: beneficiary spelling, bank branch, country, intermediary bank, currency or payment reference. Even a legitimate supplier may have changed its banking arrangement, but legitimacy must be established before funds move.

Build the control into vendor onboarding and the purchase agreement. The process should state where approved bank details are stored, who can request a change, which trusted contacts can verify it, who approves it and how long a payment hold remains in place.

## Define the controlled bank master record

The bank master is the approved payment destination linked to a verified supplier legal entity. It should not be copied casually from the latest invoice.

| Controlled field | What to compare | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier legal entity | contract, vendor record and invoice issuer | links the commercial obligation to the payee relationship |
| Beneficiary name | approved bank record and supporting evidence | unexpected differences require explanation and review |
| Bank and branch location | country, branch and routing information | a jurisdiction change can alter risk and payment handling |
| Account and routing identifiers | approved master versus change request | prevents a new destination from bypassing onboarding |
| Currency | purchase order, invoice and bank instruction | avoids unapproved conversion or settlement changes |
| Payment purpose/reference | invoice, order and milestone | supports reconciliation and avoids ambiguous allocation |
| Effective date and approver | controlled change record | proves when the instruction became valid and who released it |

Restrict who can create or edit this record. Keep a history of previous values and supporting approvals. A spreadsheet attachment exchanged by email is not a substitute for controlled vendor-master access.

## Treat every change request as unverified at first

The receiving employee should log the request without acting on it. Capture the time, channel, sender address or account, requested effective date, claimed reason, affected invoices and all changed fields. Preserve the original message and attachments according to company policy.

Apply an immediate hold to affected payments. The hold should be visible to procurement, finance and any team able to release funds. Do not let shipment urgency, early-payment discount or a threatened production stop override the verification route.

Red flags can include:

- an unexpected change close to a payment due date;
- a beneficiary or bank country unrelated to the contracted entity;
- unusual urgency or pressure to avoid normal approval;
- a request to keep the change confidential;
- altered email domains, reply-to addresses or messaging accounts;
- inconsistent invoice formatting or contact details;
- a currency change without a contract amendment; or
- instructions to split one obligation among unrelated accounts.

One red flag does not prove fraud, and the absence of obvious red flags does not prove authenticity. Both legitimate and malicious requests require the same controlled verification.

## Verify through a trusted channel already on file

Use contact information obtained before the change request: an approved supplier-master phone number, a known account manager reached through a previously established channel, or another controlled directory. Do not use the phone number or link supplied only in the change email.

During the verification, confirm the supplier identity and the complete changed instruction, not merely “Did you send an email?” A verifier can read back the supplier entity, beneficiary, bank country, currency, affected invoice and effective date. Avoid exposing more sensitive bank data than the procedure requires.

| Verification step | Acceptable evidence | Weak substitute to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Independent callback | known contact and number from approved records | number contained only in the change request |
| Entity confirmation | contract and vendor-master identity reconciled | familiar logo or signature block |
| Authorized request | named role and documented authority | an unknown employee claiming urgency |
| Supporting document review | evidence required by buyer policy | a single editable attachment |
| Internal approval | dual or role-based approval in the controlled system | informal chat acknowledgement |

If the trusted contact cannot be reached, keep the payment on hold. A delay is operationally inconvenient; an unverified transfer can be much harder to recover.

## Separate supplier verification from internal approval

Independent supplier confirmation is only one layer. The buyer must also verify that the requested change fits the contract, purchase order, invoice and internal payment authority.

Use segregation of duties where practical. The employee who enters new bank details should not be the only person who approves and releases the first payment. Finance, procurement and compliance or management roles can review different parts of the decision according to company policy.

The approval record should answer:

1. What exactly changed?
2. Which supplier entity and orders are affected?
3. Which trusted channel and contact verified the request?
4. What supporting evidence was reviewed?
5. Were contract, invoice, tax and currency details reconciled?
6. Who entered, reviewed and approved the master-data change?
7. When may payment be released?

For the first payment to a changed account, consider any additional bank or treasury control required by policy. Do not create a nominal “test payment” unless the bank and internal rules authorize it; a small transfer to the wrong beneficiary is still a transfer.

## Reconcile the order before releasing funds

A verified bank change does not make every invoice payable. Reconfirm the commercial milestone. Match the invoice to the purchase order, approved quantity, price, deposits already paid, change orders, inspection status and shipping or production evidence required by the payment terms.

| Release check | Buyer question | Evidence source |
|---|---|---|
| Order identity | Is this the correct SKU, quantity and project? | purchase order and approved change orders |
| Milestone | Has the contractual payment trigger occurred? | sample, production, inspection or shipment record |
| Amount | Are price, prior payments and credits reconciled? | invoice and payment ledger |
| Payee | Does the approved beneficiary match the released master record? | controlled vendor master |
| Currency | Is settlement currency authorized? | contract, PO and approved amendment |
| Approval | Are required buyer roles recorded? | workflow or signed control record |

## Communicate the hold without weakening the control

Tell the supplier that payment is temporarily held under standard verification policy and specify what non-sensitive evidence or contact action is needed. Avoid accusing a legitimate supplier before facts are established. At the same time, do not disclose internal security steps in enough detail to help an attacker imitate them.

Keep production and logistics decisions separate from payment authentication. If a shipment must pause, record the commercial effect and authorized next step. Do not let a logistics deadline become the reason to skip financial control.

For orders already near dispatch, use the [pre-shipment document checklist](/blog/wireless-carplay-adapter-pre-shipment-document-checklist/) to reconcile the commercial invoice, packing list, product identity and release documents. Bank verification and shipment-document verification should support one another but remain distinct controls.

## If a suspicious or incorrect payment may have occurred

Follow the buyer's incident plan immediately. Contact the bank through an official channel, notify authorized internal security, finance, legal or insurance roles, preserve records and stop additional payments. Time can matter, so the escalation contacts and authority should be defined before an incident.

Do not rely on instructions from the suspicious message about how to “reverse” or “correct” a transfer. Use independently verified bank and company channels. Avoid altering or deleting emails, attachments, logs or approval records that may be needed for investigation.

The response plan should also protect the supplier relationship: establish a clean communication channel, confirm which accounts remain affected, review other pending invoices and determine whether credentials or contact lists may have been exposed.

## Put bank-change control into the RFQ and supplier file

Procurement can reduce ambiguity before an order begins. In the supplier onboarding or RFQ process, request the legal entity name, authorized commercial contacts, finance verification contact, normal invoice channel and bank-change procedure. Define that changed details are not effective until the buyer completes its own verification and approval.

This is also a useful supplier-evaluation signal. A credible supplier should understand why a buyer will not redirect funds from an email alone. ## Supplier bank account change checklist

- Record the original request, sender, time and changed fields.
- Place affected payments on a visible hold.
- Preserve the message and attachments under company policy.
- Compare the request with the approved supplier master.
- Verify the supplier through a trusted pre-existing channel.
- Confirm legal entity, beneficiary, bank country and currency.
- Review required supporting evidence and authority.
- Separate master-data entry, approval and payment release.
- Reconcile invoice, PO, milestone, prior payments and credits.
- Escalate unusual urgency, secrecy or jurisdiction changes.
- Document the effective date and first approved payment.
- Retain the complete decision record for audit and review.

## FAQ

### Is replying to the supplier's email enough to confirm new bank details?

No. Independently contact a known supplier representative through a trusted channel already on file and follow the buyer's approval process.

### What if the supplier says payment is urgently needed for shipment?

Keep the hold until verification is complete. Record any shipment impact separately and obtain authorized commercial direction.

### Can a familiar invoice format prove the request is genuine?

No. Logos, signatures, message history and invoice formatting can be copied or altered. Verify the entity and instruction independently.

### Should the buyer make a small test transfer first?

Only if the buyer's bank and internal policy authorize that method after verification. A small unverified payment still sends funds to the stated beneficiary.

### Who should approve a supplier bank change?

Use the roles defined by the buyer's vendor-master and payment policy, ideally separating request review, data entry, approval and payment release.

## Protect the order before releasing payment

A bank-detail change is not an administrative edit; it changes where the buyer's money goes. Pause, independently verify, reconcile the commercial obligation and document approval before release.
