# Supplier Selection Matrix for Automotive Electronics: Evidence Before Scores

A supplier selection matrix helps an automotive electronics buying committee compare qualified offers against the same requirements, evidence and decision rules. Start with essential pass-or-hold gates, then score the preferences that genuinely distinguish eligible suppliers. Keep evidence confidence visible beside each rating and test whether reasonable changes in assumptions would reverse the ranking. For an importer choosing a Smart Car Screen or CarPlay AI Box partner, the highest total is useful only when the underlying product scope is comparable and the critical claims have been checked. Use the matrix to identify the next decision: approve a defined pilot, request targeted evidence, or reject an unsuitable offer. A spreadsheet total should never turn an unresolved essential requirement into an acceptable sourcing risk.

## Define the decision the matrix will support

A selection matrix is a structured comparison of candidate suppliers for a specific purchase or project. It differs from an ongoing supplier performance scorecard, which monitors an established relationship. Here the question is which candidate should advance toward a defined pilot or commercial award, and why.

Write one decision sentence before building columns: select a supplier for a private-label portable-screen pilot intended for a named channel and market. Identify the configuration, accessory bundle, support scope and expected approval stage. A decision about pilot readiness needs different evidence from a decision authorizing unrestricted production.

Assign requirements to purchasing, engineering, quality, operations and after-sales owners. Each owner should explain what a satisfactory answer would look like and what remains unverified. Resolve contradictory requirements before asking suppliers to respond. Otherwise the committee may score the same offer against several different imagined products.

This guide covers the comparison meeting after requirements and supplier responses exist. The earlier [supplier evaluation and RFQ guide](/blog/wireless-carplay-adapter-supplier-evaluation-rfq/) explains what to request. Quote normalization remains a separate task: a scoring model cannot make different package contents or delivery responsibilities commercially equivalent.

## Separate essential gates from weighted preferences

An essential gate is a requirement whose absence prevents the intended decision. Examples might include a configuration-matched sample completing the agreed critical functions, an identified production location, or an acceptable evidence package for the intended market reviewed by the responsible specialist. Define each gate precisely enough to evaluate it.

Record gate states as pass, fail or hold pending evidence. A hold means the committee cannot yet conclude whether the requirement is met. It does not imply poor capability, but it prevents an unconditional award if that evidence is essential. A feature that would be pleasant to have usually belongs among preferences instead.

[New Zealand Government Procurement's pre-condition guidance](https://www.procurement.govt.nz/government-procurement-framework/government-procurement-rules/approaching-the-market/pre-conditions/) illustrates the separation between essential eligibility and scored evaluation. That source governs its own public-procurement context. The private-sector framework here adapts the decision principle; it does not assert that those public rules govern your purchase.

For a Smart Car Screen project, mounting suitability for the agreed installation may be a gate, while the convenience of replacing a bracket may be a scored preference. For an AI Box, validate the required host and control workflow on the specified configuration. Do not substitute an attractive enclosure, an unrelated certificate or a low quotation for that evidence.

## Build an evidence ledger before assigning numbers

Give every important claim a reference: document revision, sample identity, demonstration record, named owner and review date. State what the evidence actually covers. A report on a different firmware branch may inform a question without proving that the offered build meets the requirement.

Use three separate evidence states: supplier-stated, reviewed, and independently checked within a defined scope. These are labels for the buying team's review status, not universal assurance grades. A witnessed demonstration can still be narrow; a reviewed document can still be incomplete. Record the missing conditions in ordinary language.

For example, a portable-screen supplier might demonstrate call audio on one bench setup. That supports the demonstrated path. It does not establish every vehicle's audio integration. The ledger should identify the proposed use cases and the additional confirmation needed before publishing channel claims.

The [supplier quality-system evidence review](/blog/automotive-electronics-supplier-quality-system-evidence-review/) helps buyers distinguish a documented process from evidence that it is used. Refer to that work when evaluating controls, rather than awarding points solely for the existence of a procedure title.

## Use a matrix the committee can actually explain

Keep the model small enough that every row influences a real decision. The following worksheet is a starting structure, not a prescribed industry weighting. Replace the examples with project-specific criteria and agree their importance before reviewing totals.

| Decision area | Example evidence | Treatment | Decision owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Required user workflow | Identified sample and agreed critical-function demonstration | Essential gate | Engineering |
| Approved product identity | Hardware, firmware and kit revision linked to the offer | Essential gate | Product lead |
| Production control | Traceable records showing configuration and release controls | Gate or preference according to project risk | Quality |
| Support execution | Named escalation route and example diagnostic handover | Scored preference with minimum scope if essential | After-sales |
| Delivery readiness | Material assumptions, pilot route and capacity evidence | Scored preference or essential deadline gate | Operations |
| Commercial value | Normalized offer and stated whole-life assumptions | Scored preference | Purchasing and finance |

Avoid double counting. If change control appears under engineering, quality and service, decide which row owns the primary assessment. Other owners can comment on consequences without awarding the same strength three times. Similarly, avoid giving both purchase price and a total-cost measure full weight when the latter already includes the purchase price.

Use a shared rating scale with written anchors. In an illustrative zero-to-four scale, zero might mean no acceptable response, two a partially supported response with defined gaps, and four a response meeting the specified high-performance anchor with relevant evidence. Define one and three as well. An unknown response should be marked unknown until clarified, rather than silently assigned an average score.

## Calculate transparently and test the result

For eligible suppliers, one simple method is weighted points equals weight multiplied by rating divided by the maximum rating. If weights total 100 and the maximum rating is four, a criterion weighted at 20 with a rating of three contributes 15 points. This is arithmetic for an example model, not a supplier result or recommended allocation.

[New Zealand Government Procurement's evaluation methodology guide](https://www.procurement.govt.nz/guides/guide-to-procurement/plan-your-procurement/evaluation-methodology/) describes weighted-attribute evaluation. It provides a useful methodological reference. Your committee still needs its own criterion definitions, evidence thresholds and approval authority.

Do not multiply ratings by an invented confidence percentage and present the output as a probability of success. Keep uncertainty explicit. For a weakly supported preference, record a justified rating range and calculate the resulting total range. A candidate with a narrow lead and wide unresolved range may need a focused demonstration before the committee can distinguish the offers.

Test sensitivity using plausible alternative priorities agreed by the team. Would the preferred supplier change if support carried slightly more importance and commercial value slightly less? Would correcting an uncertain delivery-readiness rating reverse the order? These are checks on robustness, not invitations to tune the weights until a favored supplier wins.

## Work through a hypothetical portable-screen decision

Imagine a regional distributor comparing two suppliers for a private-label Smart Car Screen pilot. Both submitted the same requested kit scope and passed the agreed critical-function gates. Candidate A receives a slightly higher initial total because its commercial offer is stronger. Candidate B has more complete after-sales evidence, including an identifiable diagnostic handover and clearer ownership of unresolved cases.

The committee should identify which assumption drives the difference. If A's support rating rests on a promise while B's rests on reviewed records, treating both as equally certain would obscure the decision. Ask A for the specific missing evidence under the same clarification process available to B. Keep a dated copy of the original assessment.

If a reasonable support-priority scenario changes the preferred candidate, record that dependency. The committee may defer the award for a targeted support exercise. Alternatively, it may authorize a bounded pilot once essential conditions are met, with an explicit exit decision and no implied approval for broader production. The appropriate choice depends on the project's constraints and the authority approving the exposure.

Nothing in this scenario describes a real supplier, customer outcome, price or delivery commitment. Its purpose is to show how a matrix turns an uncertain ranking into a concrete next action.

## Capture the award rationale, not just the total

Keep a short decision record beside the worksheet. It should let another manager understand the recommendation without reconstructing the meeting. Preserve dissent when it identifies a material unresolved issue; averaging opinions can conceal a substantive disagreement about evidence.

| Record field | What the committee should write |
|---|---|
| Decision boundary | Pilot, specific configuration, market and quantity scope to be confirmed |
| Eligibility | Gate outcomes and evidence references, including unresolved holds |
| Preferred candidate | Recommendation and the reasons that matter most |
| Uncertainty | Missing evidence, sensitivity outcome and assumptions that could change ranking |
| Conditions and owners | Required action, accountable owner and verification point |
| Approval and expiry | Authorized approver, date and trigger for reassessment |

Have evaluators make initial assessments separately, then moderate differences against the evidence. A product engineer and finance lead may disagree because one assumed an included accessory and the other priced it separately. Resolve that scope difference before debating numerical ratings.

Reopen affected rows when the offered configuration, production site, support scope or commercial basis changes. A supplier selected for one approved portable-screen kit has not automatically qualified every AI Box or motorcycle display in its catalog. Retain the old decision and document why the revision was necessary.

## Limits that remain after selection

The matrix supports a sourcing judgment. It does not prove universal compatibility, product reliability, future capacity or regulatory suitability. Those conclusions require the relevant verification and specialist review within their own scope. Supplier selection also does not replace sample approval, contractual agreement, shipment release or incoming acceptance.

For a straightforward repeat purchase with stable requirements, a detailed weighted model may add little value. For a new platform with unresolved essential functions, scoring may be premature. Use the simplest documented method that supports the decision and exposes consequential uncertainty.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a supplier selection matrix the same as a supplier scorecard?

They can share a format, but their purpose differs. A selection matrix compares candidates for a defined sourcing decision. An operating scorecard monitors an existing supplier over time. Set criteria and evidence around the decision being made.

### Should the cheapest supplier receive the highest total?

Only if its qualified offer performs best under the agreed evaluation method. First normalize the commercial scope and apply essential gates. Then assess relevant value, support and execution factors without counting the same cost twice.

### How should missing evidence be scored?

Mark the gap explicitly and request clarification. Hold an essential gate until acceptable evidence exists. For preferences, follow the declared rating rules and preserve uncertainty; do not replace an unknown answer with an assumed average.

### What if a small weighting change reverses the ranking?

Treat the recommendation as sensitive to that priority. Identify the disputed criterion or evidence gap, seek targeted confirmation and explain the dependency to the approver. Do not adjust weights retrospectively to manufacture a preferred result.

### Can the highest-ranked supplier move straight to production?

Only after the separately required approvals and conditions are complete. The matrix may support a pilot recommendation while sample approval, market review, contractual scope or production-release requirements remain open. State the authorized boundary explicitly.
