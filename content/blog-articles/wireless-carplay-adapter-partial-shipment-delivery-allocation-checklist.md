## Direct answer: how should a partial shipment be planned?

A wireless CarPlay adapter partial shipment should be treated as a series of controlled releases within one purchase order, not as an informal request to “send what is ready.” Before production release, the buyer and supplier should define the quantity and SKU configuration for each shipment, its readiness and inspection gate, freight responsibility, documents, payment trigger, delivery destination, traceability records and the rule for reconciling the remaining balance.

The safest plan identifies which commercial need each shipment serves—launch inventory, distributor allocation, service stock or backorder recovery—without mixing unapproved hardware, firmware, packaging or accessory revisions. Each release then receives its own acceptance evidence and shipping record while remaining linked to the master order.

## What partial shipment and delivery allocation mean

A **partial shipment**, also called a split shipment, is the release of less than the total ordered quantity, with one or more later shipments completing the order. **Delivery allocation** is the documented assignment of quantities by SKU, destination, market, warehouse, channel or business purpose.

These terms describe commercial and logistics decisions. They do not prove that a quantity has completed production, passed inspection, been handed to the carrier or arrived at the destination. Keep those milestones separate:

1. quantity allocated to a shipment;
2. production completed for that quantity;
3. inspection accepted;
4. shipment released and handed over;
5. export and import milestones completed;
6. destination receipt confirmed; and
7. master-order balance reconciled.

This distinction prevents a forecasted allocation from being reported as available stock.

## When splitting an adapter order can help

Partial shipment can support a launch when one market needs stock earlier, a downstream customer has a committed window, or one approved configuration is ready while another still requires controlled work. It can also isolate service inventory, reduce the impact of a localized freight delay or support a phased channel rollout.

However, an extra shipment is not automatically faster or cheaper. It may create another inspection event, export document set, freight minimum, customs entry, destination handling charge and receiving task. More releases also increase the chance of mixing revisions or losing sight of the unshipped balance.

Use a split only when the business value of the earlier quantity is clear and the added control and logistics cost are understood. If the first lot is too small to support a useful launch, or if packaging and firmware are still changing, a consolidated shipment may be the lower-risk choice.

## Freeze the exact configuration of every release

Do not allocate only a model family name. Freeze the saleable SKU and its controlled attributes for each shipment:

- adapter hardware revision and approved firmware build;
- housing color, surface treatment and brand artwork revision;
- cable, converter, manual and accessory set;
- unit label, serial format and regulatory marking scope;
- inner pack, retail box, carton and shipping-mark revision;
- language or market variant;
- barcode and channel label; and
- approved deviation, if any.

If the later balance will use a new revision, record the effective shipment or serial boundary. Never silently combine revisions inside one carton or present two configurations as interchangeable without buyer approval.

## Build a delivery allocation table

A single allocation table should connect demand to physical release. The quantities below are planning categories, not recommended percentages.

| Allocation purpose | Buyer decision | Supplier evidence | Release risk to control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch inventory | minimum useful quantity and required destination date | completed SKU quantity and accepted inspection record | too little stock to support the launch |
| Distributor or regional warehouse | quantity by destination and channel | carton mapping, shipping marks and destination documents | wrong market label or mixed configuration |
| Samples and marketing | approved sample status and recipients | serial list and sample designation | sample units mistaken for saleable inventory |
| Service stock | supported installed base and spare policy | segregated SKU quantity and traceability | service units consumed as launch stock |
| Backorder recovery | priority customers and allocation authority | released quantity and dispatch record | uncontrolled promises across channels |
| Remaining balance | required configuration and target window | open-order report and completion plan | forgotten or repeatedly rescheduled balance |

The buyer should own channel priorities. The supplier should not guess which warehouse or customer receives scarce early units.

## Define the release gate for shipment one

The first shipment must pass a complete gate even when later quantities remain in production. A practical release pack includes:

- approved purchase order and allocation revision;
- frozen SKU and packaging specification;
- completed quantity statement;
- agreed inspection result and disposition;
- serial or lot traceability file where required;
- carton count, weights and dimensions;
- approved commercial invoice and packing-list data;
- shipping marks and destination instructions;
- carrier booking or handover evidence; and
- authorized release approval.

Do not waive relevant acceptance checks simply because the first quantity is urgent. A partial release should not convert schedule pressure into an undocumented quality exception. ## Price the split before approving it

Compare the full landed impact of the split with consolidation. Ask which charges repeat and which apply only once.

| Cost or responsibility | Question to settle |
|---|---|
| Inspection | Is each shipment inspected separately, and who pays for repeat attendance? |
| Factory and forwarder handling | Are there repeated pickup, documentation or warehouse fees? |
| Freight | Does each release trigger a minimum charge or different mode? |
| Export documents | Which documents must be issued per shipment? |
| Customs clearance | Will each arrival create a separate entry, brokerage or duty process? |
| Destination handling | Are terminal, delivery appointment or receiving costs repeated? |
| Insurance | Is each shipment covered under the selected arrangement and declared value? |
| Repacking or relabeling | Who pays if destination allocation changes after pack-out? |

State the selected Incoterms rule, exact named place and edition on the transaction documents. Incoterms allocate defined delivery, cost and risk responsibilities; they do not determine the production-completion gate, payment acceptance or inventory allocation. Use the [Incoterms and shipping responsibility checklist](/blog/wireless-carplay-adapter-incoterms-shipping-responsibility-checklist/) to assign the physical handover correctly.

## Connect payment to evidence, not assumptions

Partial shipment often changes the cash timeline. The contract should state whether payment is calculated by the value of each accepted release, by a fixed milestone, or by another agreed method. Define the evidence required before an invoice becomes payable and how deposits, tooling, non-recurring costs, freight and the final balance are reconciled.

Avoid phrases such as “balance before shipment” when there will be several shipments. Specify which balance, which shipment, the accepted quantity and the document set. The [payment terms and milestone checklist](/blog/wireless-carplay-adapter-payment-terms-milestone-checklist/) provides a structure for connecting money to verified deliverables.

## Control documents and traceability per shipment

Give every release a unique shipment reference linked to the master purchase order. Retain the allocation revision, invoice, packing list, carton map, carrier reference, inspection decision and serial or lot record together. If several destinations are included, show which cartons and serial ranges belong to each destination.

The master order register should show:

- ordered quantity by SKU;
- quantity accepted and shipped in each release;
- quantity received or under claim;
- approved cancellations or changes;
- replacement quantity, if any; and
- open balance by SKU and destination.

This prevents the same units from being counted twice—once as shipped stock and again as an open production balance.

## Protect later shipments from uncontrolled change

A gap between releases can invite configuration drift. Apply formal change control to component substitutions, firmware updates, artwork corrections, accessory changes, carton changes and test-method revisions. Review whether a change affects units already shipped, inventory compatibility, support instructions or replacement stock.

If the buyer approves a change only for the remaining balance, identify the effective date, purchase-order line, lot or serial boundary. If rework of the first shipment is needed, define location, responsibility, validation evidence and inventory disposition before distributing those units further.

## Plan shortage, damage and replacement handling

Define how quantity differences are verified at factory dispatch and destination receipt. A claim should identify the shipment reference, SKU, carton, serial or lot where applicable, condition, evidence and requested disposition. Separate carrier damage, packing shortage, nonconforming product and buyer allocation error because the evidence and responsibility may differ.

Replacement units need their own approval and traceability. State whether they reduce the remaining order balance, are shipped as warranty replacements or create a new commercial line. Keep service inventory protected; the [after-sales service stock guide](/blog/wireless-carplay-adapter-after-sales-service-stock-planning/) helps buyers distinguish spare capacity from normal sellable allocation.

## Close the order with a final reconciliation

The final release is not complete merely because the last cartons leave the factory. Reconcile the original order, all approved revisions, every shipment, destination receipts, claims, replacements, payments and remaining materials or packaging commitments.

The closure record should answer four questions: Was every approved SKU quantity delivered? Were all deviations resolved? Do payment and document totals agree? Are any support, warranty, artwork or material obligations still open?

Only then should the order balance become zero in both buyer and supplier records.

## Buyer checklist before approving a partial shipment

- Confirm the business reason and minimum useful quantity for the early release.
- Freeze the exact SKU, hardware, firmware, accessory and packaging revision.
- Allocate quantity by destination, market, warehouse and purpose.
- Define completion, inspection, release, handover and receipt milestones separately.
- Approve the inspection plan and authorized release evidence.
- Map serial or lot traceability to cartons and destinations.
- Compare repeated freight, handling, customs and document costs.
- State the Incoterms rule, named place and edition.
- Define payment evidence for every release and the final balance.
- Control changes between the first shipment and later shipments.
- Define shortage, damage, claim and replacement treatment.
- Reconcile all quantities, documents, receipts and payments at order closure.

## FAQ

### Is a partial shipment always faster than one consolidated shipment?

No. It can release an approved quantity earlier, but repeat inspection, booking, customs and destination processes may offset the schedule benefit. Compare the complete route and business value.

### Can different firmware versions be shipped under one SKU?

Only if the configuration control and buyer approval explicitly allow it. Otherwise, freeze one approved firmware build per defined release and identify any later revision boundary.

### Who decides which distributor receives the first units?

The buyer should approve channel and destination priorities. The supplier should execute a documented allocation rather than infer commercial priority.

### Does a partial shipment change Incoterms responsibility?

Not by itself. Apply the agreed rule and named place to each shipment, while separately defining inspection, payment, title and inventory-allocation terms.

### How is the remaining purchase-order balance closed?

Reconcile ordered, shipped, received, claimed, replaced, cancelled and open quantities by SKU and destination, then match the related documents and payments before closing the order.
