# OEM/ODM Subcontracting in Automotive Electronics: Who Actually Builds Your Product?

An automotive electronics supplier may outsource assembly, programming, testing or packaging while remaining your commercial contact. Before approving that arrangement, identify the actual process locations, the responsible organizations, the configuration each receives and the evidence returned at every handoff. Approve a defined production route rather than relying on a supplier name or a sample that passed elsewhere. A capable subcontractor can be part of a sound sourcing model; the procurement risk is an undisclosed or uncontrolled boundary where requirements, material identity or release responsibility disappear. This guide helps importers and private-label sourcing managers decide whether a proposed OEM/ODM route is sufficiently transparent to approve, needs a bounded pilot, or should remain on hold. It does not assume that all work must happen inside one factory.

## Define subcontracting by the work performed

For this discussion, subcontracting means that your contracted supplier delegates a defined activity to another organization. That activity might be circuit-board assembly, enclosure production, firmware loading, screen integration, inspection or regional pack-out. A purchased component is also an external input, but it creates a different approval question from moving your finished-product programming operation to another site.

Start with a process map that names the operations and physical locations. A trading office, engineering center, assembly plant and fulfillment warehouse may all have legitimate roles. Their names alone do not establish who performs the production work. Ask what happens at each location, which entity controls it and who can retrieve its records when a shipment is questioned.

An affiliate or sister company can still create a meaningful process boundary. Shared ownership does not prove shared instructions, equipment, access permissions or traceability. Conversely, separate legal entities do not automatically mean an unreliable supply chain. Evaluate the handoff and its controls instead of drawing conclusions from an organization chart.

## Separate subcontractor approval from other sourcing decisions

This article concerns work delegated within the primary supplier's proposed route. Qualifying an alternate supply path is a different decision, covered in the [second-source qualification framework](/blog/automotive-electronics-second-source-qualification-framework/). Choosing whether evidence requires a visit is addressed by the [remote versus on-site supplier audit guide](/blog/remote-vs-onsite-supplier-audit-automotive-electronics/). Neither activity automatically approves every subcontracted operation.

| Decision | What the buyer is deciding | Evidence that helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary supplier selection | Whether the commercial and technical partner fits the project | Capability, scope, responsibility and reference evidence |
| Subcontracted route approval | Whether a named delegated operation fits the approved production route | Process/site map, requirements transfer and linked output records |
| Alternate-source qualification | Whether a separate supply path may be activated | Difference assessment, representative pilot and activation rules |
| Shipment release | Whether a particular lot meets agreed release conditions | Lot identity, required results, exceptions and authorized disposition |

One positive decision should not silently substitute for another. A subcontractor appearing on an internal approved-provider list is useful context, but the buyer still needs to understand whether that approval covers this operation, product and intended configuration.

## Ask for a route disclosure that supports a decision

Request the product family, controlled revision, operation, performing site, process owner and output sent to the next stage. Include where firmware or customer artwork is introduced and where final release is authorized. Make the scope specific enough to distinguish a Smart Car Screen accessory pack-out from display assembly, or an AI Box programming step from board manufacture.

The ISO 9001 Auditing Practices Group's [2016 guidance on external providers](https://committee.iso.org/files/live/sites/tc176/files/PDF%20APG%20New%20Disclaimer%2012-2023/ISO-TC%20176-TF_APG-ExternalProviders.pdf) discusses external inputs broadly, including outsourced activities and associated organizations, and considers approval and purchasing evidence. That historical guidance supports asking about external process controls. It is not a product certification, a current standards determination or proof that a particular supplier conforms.

The sourcing method here is an editorial decision framework. Agree the applicable customer, program and market requirements separately. Avoid asking for a blanket declaration that everything is made in-house when the important unresolved question is how a specific delegated step is controlled.

## Follow requirements through the boundary

Sending a drawing or firmware file is not the same as confirming which revision an operator used. Ask how the supplier releases requirements, how the subcontractor acknowledges them, how superseded instructions are removed and how open work is handled after a revision change. Identify who can stop work when an instruction is unclear.

For an AI Box program, the relevant boundary might include the permitted software configuration, hardware compatibility, programming job identity and treatment of failed units. For a portable screen kit, it might involve the selected mount, power accessory, printed insert and regional carton. These examples describe risks to review; they do not claim a particular TrolinkTek SKU uses a subcontracted process.

Trace one representative work order in both directions. Starting from the released requirement, find its job and output records. Starting from a finished lot, find the work order and the requirements it used. A screenshot of an approved document cannot establish that the correct version reached the production station for the selected lot.

## Use a handoff worksheet rather than a blanket assurance

Complete the following worksheet jointly with the primary supplier. Add actual evidence references and decision owners. An empty field identifies an unanswered question; it does not automatically establish a defective product. Keep confidential technical detail in a controlled record rather than a broadly circulated sales attachment.

| Handoff question | Record to request | Buyer decision enabled |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who performs this operation and where? | Named process/site map and responsible contact | Whether the proposed route matches the approved scope |
| What configuration may be processed? | Released revision, work order and acknowledgement | Whether requirements reached the intended operation |
| How is material identity preserved? | Incoming, work-in-process and output identifier links | Whether returned units can be traced to the operation |
| What proves the output is acceptable? | Defined criteria, results and exception records | Whether the next stage may accept the output |
| Who handles a mismatch? | Hold, escalation and disposition authority | Whether unresolved material can bypass controls |
| What happens if the route changes? | Notice, impact review and implementation reference | Whether the changed route needs renewed approval |

Use the worksheet to narrow the discussion. If the missing evidence concerns pack-out identity, a full radio performance retest may not answer it. If the change concerns programming equipment or software access, a carton photograph cannot close that risk. Match the next action to the unanswered question.

## Keep accountability visible when work leaves the factory

Name the primary supplier's owner for subcontractor selection, instruction transfer, output review and issue response. Then identify the counterpart who actually controls each delegated operation. The buyer needs a usable escalation path rather than a collection of company names that refer questions to one another.

Define who can accept returned work and who can authorize final shipment. A subcontractor's passing report can support a decision without becoming the sole release authority. Where an exception exists, record its affected scope, review evidence and disposition. Commercial urgency should not quietly change those decision rights.

Agree how records will be accessible during investigations and after personnel changes. Ask for a representative retrieval demonstration, with unrelated customer information removed. A contractual promise to provide records has practical value only if the responsible teams can identify and retrieve the records that connect the relevant lot and operation.

## Protect access without losing necessary visibility

Some route information involves commercial confidentiality or proprietary process detail. Decide what the buyer actually needs: a site identity, a bounded capability review, redacted evidence or observation of a selected operation. Avoid demanding unrelated customer drawings or complete unrestricted repositories as the default response.

WIPO's [trade secret overview](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/trade-secrets) describes limited access and reasonable protection measures as important features of confidential business information. For project planning, use that principle to discuss authorized recipients and the minimum information each operation needs. Specific rights and obligations need appropriate contractual and jurisdictional review.

Distinguish read access from permission to modify, copy, reprogram or redistribute. Where firmware, customer artwork or device credentials move across the boundary, identify the authorized transfer method and the handling of superseded material. Do not exchange production secrets or personal customer data merely to complete a sourcing questionnaire.

## An illustrative supplier-review decision

Consider a hypothetical importer preparing a private-label Smart Car Screen launch. The supplier assembles the display at its main site but proposes a separate partner for regional accessory kits. The engineering sample passed with one mount and power cable. The commercial team now wants several regional bundles.

The committee's first question should be whether the delegated pack-out route preserves the approved bundle identity. Request a representative kit order, the issued bill of materials, accessory identification, output record and exception handling. Confirm where a wrong insert or cable would be detected and who can stop the affected kits from shipping. Repeating a display startup demonstration would not establish this handoff.

If the records join and the proposed operation can reproduce the intended kit, the buyer can define a bounded pilot and its acceptance evidence. If accessory substitutions remain uncontrolled, keep that route approval open while other project work proceeds. Neither outcome proves all future lots acceptable; routine controls and shipment decisions still apply.

This scenario is illustrative, not a customer case or a statement about an existing production arrangement. It shows how procurement, engineering, quality and operations can focus on one decision instead of treating subcontracting as either automatically unacceptable or automatically approved.

## Decide what changes require another review

Agree notification and review triggers before routine production. Potential triggers include another site, a changed delegated operation, different programming access, revised inspection responsibility or an additional tier of outsourcing. The appropriate response depends on the affected risk and the approval conditions already agreed.

A route change can have consequences for product evidence, customer requirements or market documentation. Ask qualified teams to assess those consequences for the actual configuration. This guide does not establish that any change is automatically permitted or that existing regulatory evidence automatically transfers to another site.

Keep a dated decision record: proposed route, evidence reviewed, exclusions, unresolved questions, authorized scope and conditions for reopening the decision. Connect any product or process revision to the [OEM/ODM engineering change impact review](/blog/oem-odm-engineering-change-request-impact-review/) rather than approving it through an informal message.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is subcontracting a reason to reject a supplier?

Not by itself. Assess the actual operation, visibility, capability, requirements transfer and release controls. An undisclosed critical boundary deserves investigation; a disclosed and controlled route can support a sourcing decision within its evaluated scope.

### Does an approved sample approve every production site?

No. Establish where and how the sample was made and whether it represents the proposed route. Define any additional process evidence or pilot needed before accepting a different production arrangement.

### Must buyers audit every subcontractor on site?

There is no universal answer here. Choose the evidence method according to risk, access, applicable requirements and unanswered questions. Remote records, live demonstrations and site visits provide different kinds of assurance.

### Who should answer when a subcontracted operation fails?

Agree the primary supplier's coordination owner and the delegated process owner before release. Preserve the affected identifiers and evidence, then use the agreed containment and disposition process rather than allowing responsibility to circulate between companies.

### What belongs in an initial OEM/ODM inquiry?

Include product family, proposed customization, destination markets, delegated operations already known and the decision you need to make. Ask for a scoped production-route discussion and factory profile before assuming which work is performed at which site.
